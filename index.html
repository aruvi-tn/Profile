CyberMaster3D Heavy Lift, Anti-Heeling and Draft Survey

2025 to present
In service on board. The owner has ordered it for two more vessels.

On a heavy-lift offshore vessel, the main cranes were run separately from the loadicator. I added three modules to CyberMaster3D and connected them to live draft and tank sensors, so the crew see the whole vessel on one screen and plan a lift before it happens.

  • I designed the logic, the crane centre-of-gravity algorithm and the interface, and led the software team through delivery.
  • The crew plan a lift, see its effect on stability and get the ballast calculated before lifting, including the case where the load is suddenly lost.
  • The draft survey module locates unknown weights, including flooding, from measured drafts.

SUMA EFE, fuel and emissions optimisation

Nov 2024 to Mar 2026, Innovate UK Smart Shipping Acceleration Fund
Lloyd’s Register type approval, March 2026. Project end report modelled 2 to 6% fuel savings.

Masters of controllable-pitch propeller vessels often run them like fixed-pitch ones, because the best pitch for the conditions is hard to find. SUMA reads wind, waves and current, works out resistance and power demand, and recommends the pitch and engine RPM that use the least fuel.

  • I took an early Cybermarine idea and made it a product: architecture, technical features, logic, interface and specification. I led the developers through testing.
  • Under the Innovate UK SSAF project it moved to a machine-learning model as SUMA EFE. I was on board the research vessel Prince Madog for inspection and installation, and Innovate UK signed off the end report.
  • I led the product through Lloyd’s Register type approval.

G-Mod, grain stability for existing loadicators

Cybermarine
Delivered across a 47-vessel bulk carrier programme, most already class approved.

When class amended the grain stability rules for bulk carriers, owners did not want to replace the loadicators already on board. I led Cybermarine’s grain stability work: I studied the amendment, built tools to calculate the amended moments and developed G-Mod, a grain stability version of CyberMaster.

G-Mod imports condition reports from the vessel’s existing loadicator, rebuilds each loading condition, checks it against the amended grain rules and produces a new report. Owners stay compliant without new hardware.

Stability booklet automation

Cybermarine
Quicker, consistent revisions with the right criteria applied every time.

Stability booklets were assembled by hand. It was slow, criteria were sometimes missed or applied wrongly, and every revision brought the same errors back. I wrote the specification and logic for a tool that automates it, and the software team built it.

It takes the vessel’s inputs and selects the applicable stability criteria automatically, and keeps models, run files and loading conditions in one place with shared storage.

JoRes4 tug digital hull geometry

Dec 2024, University of Southampton
Published as open CFD validation data on jores.net.

I converted a dry-dock 3D laser scan of the JoRes4 tug into a clean, CFD-ready hull geometry, and found and corrected a propeller asymmetry against the as-designed geometry.

It was published with co-authors from the JoRes project, the University of Southampton and Wärtsilä.

I have also added passenger vessel damage stability to CyberMaster, and lead CyberMaster3D deliveries and new modules.

DSV Jana 201

Independent roll reduction and motion control study2026
ClientJANA Marine Services, Khobar
ToolsHydroD, Sesam GeniE

A diving support vessel under construction showed unusually high roll and pitch in moderate seas. I ran linear diffraction analysis for roll response, natural periods, roll stiffness and damping, reviewed the shipyard’s predictions and loading conditions, and assessed operability with moonpool and heading effects.

The report recommended loading changes for GM and roll stiffness, heading selection, bilge keel optimisation and passive stabilisation options.

Papers and research

Conference papers and research that came out of the product and engineering work.

  1. SMATECH 2026, LondonApril 2026, invited paper

    An integrated approach to energy optimisation: optimal steaming with controllable pitch propulsion

    Finds a vessel’s optimum power demand for a given speed and sea state, then the propeller RPM and pitch combination with the lowest fuel use. A case study on a 40 m wind farm service vessel showed a 22% cut in fuel per nautical mile, plus 1.2% from optimised pitch. Closely linked to SUMA.

    Lead author, with M R Kaushik Shiva and Dr Sudheesh Ramadasan
  2. SMATECH 2026, LondonApril 2026, invited paper

    Ensuring fatigue life of WAPS foundations

    An ABS-compliant fatigue assessment for wind-assisted propulsion foundations, combining aerodynamic block loading with probabilistic wave loads. On a 24 m eSAIL foundation on MR product tankers, it showed the existing structure could carry the sail for 25 years without reinforcement, saving steel, retrofit cost and dry-dock time.

    Lead author, with Bikash Gupta and Dr Sudheesh Ramadasan
  3. MARINE 2025, EdinburghJune 2025

    How to use JoRes ship-scale open data for CFD validation?

    Introduces the JoRes open ship-scale validation dataset of seven vessels, discusses the results and suggests how best to use them to validate CFD methods. Presented in the full-scale ship performance session.

    Co-author, with Dmitry Ponkratov and Miles Wheeler of Siemens Digital Industries Software
  4. University of SouthamptonMSc thesis, September 2024

    Numerical investigation of the performance of ships with wind-assisted propulsion

    A coupled hull and Flettner-rotor CFD study in Star-CCM+, showing speed gains of about 24% at low speed and 5.5% at design speed.

    Supervised by Prof Tahsin Tezdogan
